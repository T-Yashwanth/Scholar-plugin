# APA 7th Edition Reference - Student Papers

The full APA 7 rule set for this plugin. The drafting skills write to it and
the verifier checks against it. Student paper format only, not
professional manuscripts.

## Sources for these rules

- American Psychological Association. (2020). *Publication manual of the
  American Psychological Association* (7th ed.).
  https://doi.org/10.1037/0000165-000
- APA Style website: https://apastyle.apa.org
- Purdue Online Writing Lab. *APA formatting and style guide (7th
  edition)*. Purdue University.
  https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/general_format.html
  (see also the Reference List: Basic Rules, Author/Authors, Articles in
  Periodicals, Books, Other Print Sources, Electronic Sources, Audiovisual
  Media, Other Non-Print Sources, and In-Text Citations pages)

Precedence:

1. The professor's instructions win over everything in this file.
2. This file covers the common cases. When a case is not covered here,
   follow the Publication Manual.

## How to read the examples

- `*text*` means italic. The Word script turns `*text*` into italics.
- APA uses an en dash in page and date ranges (45-60). This plugin bans en
  dashes, so ranges use a hyphen everywhere.
- Capital letters in patterns (A. A., E. E.) stand for real initials.

# Part 1. Paper format

## Page setup

- US Letter, 8.5 x 11 inches, 1 inch margins on all sides.
- Double-spaced everywhere, including the title page and references. No
  extra space before or after paragraphs.
- Left-aligned text with a ragged right edge. No full justification.
- First line of every body paragraph indented 0.5 inch.

## Font

Use one font consistently. APA accepts any of:

- Times New Roman 12 pt (this plugin's default)

## Page numbers and running head

- Page number in the top right corner of every page, starting at 1 on the
  title page.
- Student papers have no running head. Page number only.

## Student title page

Centered, in the upper half of page 1, three or four lines down:

1. Paper title: bold, title case. It may run to two lines.
2. One blank double-spaced line.
3. Author name: first name, middle initial, last name. No titles or degrees.
4. Department and university, for example `School of Business, University
   Name`
5. Course number and name, for example `BUS 741: Strategic Leadership`
6. Instructor name, in the form the instructor prefers
7. Due date of this assignment, taken from the prompt or the chat, with the
   month written out. Example of the format: `October 3, 2026`

## Abstract

Not required for student papers unless the professor asks. If asked: new
page, the label `Abstract` centered and bold, one paragraph without an
indent, usually under 250 words.

## Body

- Start on a new page after the title page (or abstract).
- Repeat the paper title at the top of the first body page, centered and
  bold. The Word script in this plugin does this automatically.
- An introduction does not get an "Introduction" heading.

## Headings

Use only as many levels as needed, in order. Title case for levels 1 to 3.

| Level | Format |
|---|---|
| 1 | Centered, Bold, Title Case. Text starts as a new indented paragraph. |
| 2 | Flush Left, Bold, Title Case. Text starts as a new indented paragraph. |
| 3 | Flush Left, Bold Italic, Title Case. Text starts as a new indented paragraph. |
| 4 | Indented, Bold, Title Case, Ending With a Period. Text continues on the same line. |
| 5 | Indented, Bold Italic, Title Case, Ending With a Period. Text continues on the same line. |

## Lists

- In running text, a series uses (a), (b), (c).
- Bullet or numbered lists only if the assignment allows them. Discussion
  posts use no lists unless the prompt requires them.

## Numbers

Words for zero to nine, numerals for 10 and above. Always numerals for
percentages, ages, times, dates, money, scores, and statistics (5%, 3
years old). Words for a number that starts a sentence.

# Part 2. In-text citations

## Basics

- Author-date: author's last name and year for every source used,
  whether quoted, paraphrased, or summarized.
- **Parenthetical:** `(Jones, 1998)`
- **Narrative:** `Jones (1998) found that...`
- If the author and year are both in the sentence, no parentheses are
  needed: `In 1998, Jones found that...`
- Signal phrase verbs: past tense or present perfect (`found`, `has
  argued`) when describing research.

## Quotations

- **Page numbers are required for every direct quote:** `(Jones, 1998,
  p. 199)`, or `pp. 199-201` for a range.
- **Under 40 words:** in double quotation marks, inside the sentence. The
  citation comes right after the quote; the period comes after the
  citation: `"quoted words" (Jones, 1998, p. 199).`
- **40 words or more:** block quote. New line, whole block indented 0.5
  inch, no quotation marks, double-spaced. The citation comes after the
  final period: `...end of quote. (Jones, 1998, p. 199)`
- **No page numbers** (webpages, some ebooks): use a paragraph number, a
  section name, or both: `(Smith, 1997, para. 6)` or `(Smith, 1997, Mind
  Over Matter section, para. 6)`. For audio or video, use a timestamp:
  `(Tasty, 2018, 2:15)`.
- Quote sparingly. Paraphrase is preferred in APA.

## Paraphrase and summary

- Author and year required. Page or paragraph number is optional but
  encouraged, especially for long works.
- Must be genuinely reworded. Changing a few words is not a paraphrase.

## Authors in citations

| Case | Parenthetical | Narrative |
|---|---|---|
| One author | (Ahmed, 2016) | Ahmed (2016) |
| Two authors, every time | (Wegener & Petty, 1994) | Wegener and Petty (1994) |
| Three or more, from the first citation | (Kernis et al., 1993) | Kernis et al. (1993) |
| Group author, no abbreviation | (Stanford University, 2020) | Stanford University (2020) |
| Group author, first citation | (Mothers Against Drunk Driving [MADD], 2000) | Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD, 2000) |
| Group author, later citations | (MADD, 2000) | MADD (2000) |
| No author: article, chapter, webpage | ("Using Citations," 2001) | "Using Citations" (2001) |
| No author: book, report, standalone work | (*Title of Book*, 2001) | *Title of Book* (2001) |
| No date | (Smith, n.d.) | Smith (n.d.) |
| No author and no date | ("Tutoring and APA," n.d.) | "Tutoring and APA" (n.d.) |
| Same last name, different authors | (E. Johnson, 2001; L. Johnson, 1998) | E. Johnson (2001) |
| Same author, same year | (Berndt, 1981a, 1981b) | Berndt (1981a) |

Rules behind the table:

- Ampersand `&` only inside parentheses. `and` in running text.
- `et al.` has a period after `al` only.
- Titles in citations use title case. Italicize titles of standalone works
  (books, reports, webpages); put titles of parts of works (articles,
  chapters, webpages within a site) in double quotation marks.
- Use `Anonymous` as the author only if the work is signed that way.

## Several sources in one parenthesis

- Order them as in the reference list (alphabetical by first author),
  separated by semicolons: `(Berndt, 2002; Harlow, 1983)`
- Same author, different years, oldest first: `(Deci, 1975, 1990)`

## Secondary sources

When you read about a work in another source and did not read the original:

- In text: `Johnson argued that... (as cited in Smith, 2003, p. 102)`
- Only the source you read (Smith, 2003) goes in the reference list.
- Use sparingly. Cite the original if you can get it.

## Personal communications

Emails, unrecorded conversations, unrecorded lectures, and private messages
that the reader cannot retrieve:

- In text only: `(E. Robbins, personal communication, January 4, 2001)`
- **Not** in the reference list.

## Classroom sources

- Lecture slides, notes, or recordings posted in the course site count as
  retrievable by the class. Cite them normally and list them in the
  references (see Part 4, Classroom resources).
- A live lecture that was not recorded or posted is a personal
  communication.

# Part 3. Reference list rules

## Page layout

- Starts on a new page. Label `References` centered and bold at the top.
- Double-spaced, no extra space between entries.
- **Hanging indent:** first line flush left, every following line indented
  0.5 inch.
- Every in-text citation has a reference entry, and every reference entry
  is cited in the text. Exceptions: personal communications and secondary
  originals (in text only).

## Order

- Alphabetical by the first author's last name, letter by letter. "Nothing
  precedes something": `Brown, J.` before `Browning, A.`
- Same first author:
  - one-author entries first, oldest to newest;
  - then multi-author entries, alphabetical by the second author's last
    name (then the third, and so on).
- Same author(s), same year: order by title alphabetically (ignore `A`,
  `An`, `The`) and add a, b, c after the year: `(2004a)`, `(2004b)`. Use
  the same letters in the in-text citations.
- Group authors are alphabetized by the first significant word of the name.
- No-author works are alphabetized by the first significant word of the
  title.

## Authors

- Last name, then initials with periods and a space between them:
  `Smith, J. M.`
- Separate authors with commas. An ampersand before the last author:
  `Wegener, D. T., & Petty, R. E.`
- **1 to 20 authors:** list all of them.
- **21 or more:** list the first 19, then an ellipsis (...), then the final
  author. No ampersand before the final author.
- **Group author:** spell out the full name: `American Psychological
  Association.` When a department of a larger agency is the author, the
  parent agency goes in the publisher position.
- **No author:** move the title to the author position, followed by the
  date.
- **Editor as author:** `Editor, E. E. (Ed.).` or `(Eds.)` for more than
  one.
- The author element ends with a period.

## Date

- `(Year).` for journals and books.
- `(Year, Month Day).` for newspapers, magazines, webpages, blog posts,
  social media, and videos: `(2019, March 7).`
- `(n.d.).` when no date is given.
- Retrieval date only for content designed to change over time (a live
  webpage without an archive, a social media profile): `Retrieved Month
  Day, Year, from URL`.

## Titles

- **Standalone works** (books, reports, webpages, films, videos, whole
  websites): italic, sentence case.
- **Parts of works** (journal articles, book chapters, entries in reference
  works, podcast episodes): plain, sentence case. No italics, no quotation
  marks.
- **Sentence case:** capitalize only the first word of the title, the first
  word after a colon, and proper nouns. `Deep blue: The mysteries of the
  Marianas Trench`.
- **Periodical names** (journals, magazines, newspapers): italic, title
  case, full name, with the journal's own capitalization and punctuation:
  `*Past & Present*`.
- **Descriptions in square brackets** go right after the title, not
  italic: `[Video]`, `[PowerPoint slides]`, `[Unpublished manuscript]`.

## Source (publisher, periodical, and links)

- Books: publisher name only. No location. Leave out words like `Inc.` or
  `Ltd.`. If the publisher is also the author, leave the publisher out.
- Journal articles: `*Journal Name, volume*(issue), pages.` The journal name
  and the volume are italic. The issue number and parentheses are not.
- **DOI:** include one for every work that has it, print or online, as a
  full link: `https://doi.org/10.xxxx/yyyy`. No "DOI:" label.
- **URL:** when there is no DOI, and the work is online outside an academic
  database. No "Retrieved from" unless a retrieval date is needed.
- **Article from a library database with no DOI:** no URL and no database
  name. End after the page numbers.
- **No period after a DOI or URL.** Do not break or shorten links.

# Part 4. Reference formats by source type

## Journals, magazines, newspapers

**Journal article (DOI)**
Author, A. A., Author, B. B., & Author, C. C. (Year). Title of article. *Title of Periodical, volume*(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xx.xxx/yyyy

Scruton, R. (1996). The eclipse of listening. *The New Criterion, 15*(3), 5-13.

Baniya, S., & Weech, S. (2019). Data and experience design: Negotiating community-oriented digital research with service-learning. *Purdue Journal of Service-Learning and International Engagement, 6*(1), 11-16. https://doi.org/10.5703/1288284316979

**Journal article, stable URL (no DOI, not from a library database)**
Denny, H., Nordlof, J., & Salem, L. (2018). "Tell me exactly what it was that I was doing that was so bad": Understanding the needs and expectations of working-class students in writing centers. *Writing Center Journal, 37*(1), 67-98. https://www.jstor.org/stable/26537363

**Article with an article number instead of pages**
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Name, volume*(issue), Article 123. https://doi.org/xx.xxx/yyyy

**Magazine article**
Peterzell, J. (1990, April). Better late than never. *Time, 135*(17), 20-21.

**Newspaper article, print**
Schultz, S. (2005, December 28). Calls made to strengthen state energy policies. *The Country Today*, 1A, 2A.

**Newspaper article, online**
Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of article. *Newspaper Name*. URL

**Review**
Baumeister, R. F. (1993). Exposing the self-knowledge myth [Review of the book *The self-knower: A hero under control*, by R. A. Wicklund & M. Eckert]. *Contemporary Psychology, 38*(5), 466-467.

## Books and parts of books

**Book**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work: Capital letter also for subtitle*. Publisher. https://doi.org/xx.xxx/yyyy

**Edited book, no author**
Editor, E. E. (Ed.). (Year). *Title of work: Capital letter also for subtitle*. Publisher.

**Edited book with an author**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work* (E. Editor, Ed.). Publisher.

**Edition other than the first**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work* (2nd ed.). Publisher.

**Translation**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work* (T. Translator, Trans.). Publisher. (Original work published Year)
In text: (Author, Original Year/Year)

**Multivolume work**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work* (Vol. 2). Publisher.

**Chapter in an edited book**
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor & F. F. Editor (Eds.), *Title of work* (pp. 10-25). Publisher. https://doi.org/xx.xxx/yyyy

**Ebook:** same as a print book. Leave out the format, platform, or device
(Kindle, etc.). Add the DOI or URL if there is one.

**Entry in a reference work (dictionary, encyclopedia)**
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of entry. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), *Title of reference work* (2nd ed., Vol. 1, pp. 10-12). Publisher.

Online and updated (group author, no date):
Organization Name. (n.d.). Title of entry. In *Title of reference work*. Retrieved Month Day, Year, from URL

## Reports and gray literature

**Report by an organization or government agency**
Organization Name. (Year). *Title of report* (Report No. 123). URL

**Report with individual authors**
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). *Title of report*. Organization Name. URL

**Dissertation or thesis, from a database**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of dissertation* [Doctoral dissertation, University Name]. Database Name.

**Dissertation or thesis, unpublished**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of dissertation* [Unpublished doctoral dissertation]. University Name.

**Statute (US federal)**
Name of Act, Pub. L. No. xxx-xxx, xx Stat. xxx (Year). URL

## Conference and unpublished works

**Conference presentation**
Presenter, A. A., & Presenter, B. B. (Year, Month Day). *Title of presentation* [Paper presentation]. Name of Conference, City, State, Country. URL

**Paper in published proceedings:** cite as a chapter in an edited book, or
as a journal article if the proceedings are published in a journal.

**Unpublished manuscript**
Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of manuscript* [Unpublished manuscript]. Department, University.

## Webpages and online works

**Webpage, individual author**
Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *Title of page*. Site Name. URL

**Webpage, group author** (leave out Site Name when it is the same as the author)
Group Name. (Year, Month Day). *Title of page*. URL

**Webpage, no author**
*Title of page*. (Year, Month Day). Site Name. URL

**Webpage, no date, content that changes**
Group Name. (n.d.). *Title of page*. Retrieved Month Day, Year, from URL

**Blog post**
Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of post. *Blog Name*. URL

## Audiovisual media

**YouTube or other online video**
Uploader Name. (Year, Month Day). *Title of video* [Video]. YouTube. URL

Tasty. (2018, March 7). *7 recipes you can make in 5 minutes* [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9_5wHw6l11o

If the uploader's real name and username differ: `Lastname, F. [Username].`

**TED Talk**
Speaker, A. A. (Year, Month). *Title of talk* [Video]. TED Conferences. URL

**Film**
Director, D. D. (Director). (Year). *Title of film* [Film]. Production Company.

**TV series episode**
Writer, W. W. (Writer), & Director, D. D. (Director). (Year, Month Day). Title of episode (Season 1, Episode 2) [TV series episode]. In P. P. Producer (Executive Producer), *Title of series*. Production Company.

**Podcast episode**
Host, H. H. (Host). (Year, Month Day). Title of episode (No. 12) [Audio podcast episode]. In *Title of Podcast*. Production Company. URL

**Webinar, recorded**
Presenter, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *Title of webinar* [Webinar]. Host Organization. URL

## Social media

Use the first 20 words of the post as the title, in italics. Keep emojis
and hashtags.

**Post on X (Twitter)**
Author, A. A. [@username]. (Year, Month Day). *First 20 words of the post* [Post]. X. URL

**Facebook post**
Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *First 20 words of the post* [Status update]. Facebook. URL

**Instagram post**
Author, A. A. [@username]. (Year, Month Day). *First 20 words of the caption* [Photograph]. Instagram. URL

**LinkedIn post**
Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *First 20 words of the post* [Post]. LinkedIn. URL

## Classroom resources

These are for sources posted in the course site (Canvas, Blackboard,
Moodle). APA says: when the paper is for the same class, cite the course
site name and the URL of its login page. If the professor says to cite
course materials differently, follow the professor.

**Lecture slides or notes**
Instructor, A. A. (Year). *Title of the slides* [PowerPoint slides]. Course Site Name. https://login-page-of-the-course-site

**Recorded lecture or lecture video**
Instructor, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *Title of the lecture* [Video]. Course Site Name. https://login-page-of-the-course-site

**Lecture transcript posted in the course site**
Instructor, A. A. (Year, Month Day). *Title of the lecture* [Lecture transcript]. Course Site Name. https://login-page-of-the-course-site

**Course reading posted in the course site:** cite the original work (the
journal article, the book chapter) as if you found it in its original
place. Do not cite the course site as the source of a published reading.

**Unrecorded live lecture:** personal communication, in text only (see
Part 2).

## Not in the reference list

- Personal communications: emails, conversations, unrecorded lectures,
  private messages
- The original work in an "as cited in" citation
- Unpublished interviews you conducted (describe them in the text)
- Standard software and apps (Word, Excel, R) named in passing
- Classic religious works and major classical works (in text only)

# Part 5. Checklist of common errors

In text:

- A citation in the text with no reference entry, or the reverse
- An author name or year that does not match the reference entry
- `et al.` used for a two-author source; both names are required every time
- An ampersand in narrative text, or `and` inside parentheses
- A direct quote with no page, paragraph, or timestamp
- A block quote (40+ words) in quotation marks, or with the citation
  before the final period
- Several sources in one parenthesis not in alphabetical order
- A personal communication in the reference list

In the reference list:

- Not alphabetical, or no hanging indent, or not double-spaced
- An article or book title in title case instead of sentence case
- A journal name in sentence case, or not italic
- Volume number not italic, or issue number italic
- An article title in italics or quotation marks
- `Retrieved from` added where no retrieval date is needed
- A DOI missing when the work has one, or in the old `doi:` format
- A period after a DOI or URL
- Publisher location included (not used in APA 7)
- More than 20 authors listed in full, or an ampersand after the ellipsis
- Anything made up: a DOI, page number, date, or source. Never fabricate.

## Punctuation rule for this plugin

No output from this plugin may contain an em dash (U+2014) or an en dash
(U+2013). Use a colon, a period, or a restructured sentence instead. In
page and date ranges, use a hyphen. This applies to body text, title pages,
and references.
